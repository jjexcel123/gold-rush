Chinese Migration on the Australian Goldfields
Jachin Saxby - Year 9 History Task #2

Introduction
"In the 1800s, Chinese people came to Australia in their thousands, following the lure of gold."
"The Chinese who arrived in the Australian colonies – whether in Van Diemen's Land (Tasmania), Victoria, New South Wales, Queensland and elsewhere – were mainly rural labourers. Most came from southern China, especially Guangdong Province. Many of the rural population suffered poverty, violence and instability during the 19th century, notably during the Taiping Rebellion (1851–64), which caused an estimated 20 million deaths."
"By the mid-1850s, the Chinese formed about 20 per cent of miners on the NSW and Victorian goldfields."
Causes of Migration
"In 1851 the discovery of gold brought a rush of fortune seekers from around the world."
"Men left southern China hoping to make their fortunes and support families at home who stayed behind. A man could take years to save the cost of his ticket – usually paid for by agents or family collectives, with the debts to be repaid from gold receipts."
"Push factors included famine, poverty, and civil war in China."
Experiences of Chinese Migrants
"Chinese miners formed their own camps, gave each other mutual support and worked cooperatively on the diggings."
"They brought with them their language, food, beliefs and traditions and grouped together for safety and companionship."
"Chinese miners faced discrimination, violence and special taxes imposed on them by colonial governments."
"Racist violence, including at Lambing Flat, left deep scars. In 1861, a mob of around 2000 attacked Chinese miners at Lambing Flat, driving them off the diggings and destroying their camps."
Impacts on Australian Society
"Despite the hardships, many Chinese lived on in Australia after the gold rushes, turning to storekeeping, furniture making, market gardening and banana growing."
"The Chinese contribution to the development of Australia – through agriculture, commerce and community – is widely recognised today."
Perspectives on the Goldfields
"European miners and settlers often viewed the Chinese as competition, leading to resentment and at times open hostility."
"The Chinese created their own community networks, and their presence contributed to the multicultural character of Australia."
Conclusion
"The legacy of the Chinese on the Australian goldfields is visible in the descendants of these communities and in the ongoing ties between China and Australia today."
